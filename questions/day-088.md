# SQL Scenario-Based Interview Question: Identifying Problematic Split Deliveries in E-commerce

## Business Scenario
A leading e-commerce retailer relies heavily on its internal logistics network to fulfill customer orders. The Operations team is observing a noticeable increase in customer service inquiries related to "split deliveries" where different items from the same order arrive on separate days, sometimes with significant delays between the first and last package. This leads to customer dissatisfaction, negative reviews, and increased operational costs due to follow-up support.

The Head of Operations needs a report to identify problematic orders that demonstrate a significant split delivery pattern. They consider an order problematic if:
1.  The order originally contained more than one distinct item.
2.  All items belonging to that order were eventually marked as 'DELIVERED'.
3.  The time difference between the delivery date of the *first* item and the delivery date of the *last* item for that same order is 3 days or more.

Your task as a Data Analyst is to identify these problematic `order_id`s along with the date of the first item's delivery and the date of the last item's delivery for each.

Assume:
```sql
CURRENT_DATE = '2023-10-26'
```

# Task
Write a SQL query that identifies `order_id`s that meet the "problematic split delivery" criteria.
The criteria are:
1.  The order must have more than one distinct `item_id`.
2.  All `item_id`s associated with the order must have at least one 'DELIVERED' event.
3.  The difference between the earliest delivery date (for any item in the order) and the latest delivery date (for any item in the order) must be 3 days or more.
    *Note: For an `item_id`, its delivery date is the earliest `event_timestamp` where `event_type = 'DELIVERED'` for that `item_id`.*
    *Assume a `DATE_DIFF(date_part, start_date, end_date)` function is available to calculate the difference in days between two dates. For example: `DATE_DIFF(DAY, date1, date2)` or `DATEDIFF(day, date1, date2)`.*

Your query should return the `order_id`, the `first_item_delivery_date` (earliest delivery date across all items in the order), and the `last_item_delivery_date` (latest delivery date across all items in the order).
Order the results by `order_id`.

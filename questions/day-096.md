# SQL Scenario-Based Interview Question: Customer Re-engagement Gap Analysis

## Business Scenario
An E-commerce company wants to deeply understand its customer churn and re-engagement patterns. As a Data Analyst, you've been tasked with identifying customers who have had multiple distinct subscription periods (i.e., they subscribed, unsubscribed, and then subscribed again after a break). For these re-engaging customers, the business wants to measure the "re-engagement gap" – the duration in days between the end of their previous consolidated subscription period and the start of their new one. This analysis will help in optimizing marketing campaigns for win-back strategies.

Assume:
```sql
CURRENT_DATE = '2023-10-26'
```

# Task
Your task is to:
1.  **Consolidate Subscriptions:** First, identify and consolidate overlapping or contiguous subscriptions for each customer into distinct, continuous "consolidated subscription periods". A subscription is contiguous if its `start_date` is on or before the `end_date` of the previous subscription. If an `end_date` is `NULL`, it represents an ongoing subscription; for comparison purposes during consolidation, treat its end as `CURRENT_DATE`. However, for the final `consolidated_end_date` of a period, if any underlying subscription within that period had a `NULL` end_date, the `consolidated_end_date` for the entire period should be `NULL`. Otherwise, it should be the maximum `end_date` in that consolidated period.
2.  **Calculate Re-engagement Gap:** For each customer who has *more than one* such consolidated period, calculate the 're-engagement gap' in days. The gap is the number of days between the `consolidated_end_date` of a previous period and the `consolidated_start_date` of the subsequent period.
3.  **Output:** Return `customer_id`, the `consolidated_start_date` of the re-engagement period, the `consolidated_end_date` of the previous period, and `re_engagement_gap_days`. Only show rows where a positive `re_engagement_gap_days` exists.

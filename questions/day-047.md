# SQL Scenario-Based Interview Question: SaaS Customer Re-Subscription Gap Analysis

## Business Scenario
A SaaS company is focused on improving customer retention and understanding churn behavior. As a Data Analyst, you've been tasked with identifying customers who have previously cancelled their subscriptions but later re-subscribed. The goal is to calculate the duration of their 'churned' period (the gap between their previous subscription ending and their new subscription starting) to understand re-engagement patterns. This analysis helps the marketing and sales teams target "at-risk" customers more effectively and tailor re-engagement campaigns.

Assume:
```sql
CURRENT_DATE = '2024-01-01'
```

# Task
Write a SQL query to identify all instances where a customer has re-subscribed after previously cancelling (i.e., their `end_date` was not NULL and they later started a new subscription). For each such re-subscription event, calculate the number of days the customer was 'churned' (the gap between their previous `end_date` and the `start_date` of their new subscription).

The output should include:
- `customer_id`
- `previous_subscription_end_date` (the `end_date` of the subscription immediately prior to the re-subscription)
- `re_subscription_start_date` (the `start_date` of the new subscription)
- `churn_gap_days` (the duration in days between `previous_subscription_end_date` and `re_subscription_start_date`)
- `re_subscription_number` (a sequential number indicating if it's the 1st, 2nd, etc., re-subscription for that specific customer)

Exclude any entries where `end_date` is `NULL` from the calculation of previous `end_date`, as these represent active subscriptions, not completed churn cycles. The `churn_gap_days` should be calculated as `DATEDIFF(re_subscription_start_date, previous_subscription_end_date)`.

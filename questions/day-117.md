# SQL Scenario-Based Interview Question: E-commerce Customer Re-engagement Analysis

## Business Scenario
An E-commerce company's marketing team wants to understand customer re-engagement patterns to optimize their retention strategies. They define a customer as "re-engaged" if they place an order (`re_engagement_order`) after a period of at least 90 days since their *immediately preceding* order. For each instance of re-engagement, the team wants to assess its success: a re-engagement is considered 'successful' if the value of the `re_engagement_order` is greater than or equal to the customer's average order value from *all their previous orders* (i.e., all orders placed before the `re_engagement_order_date`). This analysis helps in understanding the impact of re-engagement campaigns and identifying customers with evolving purchasing behaviors.

Assume:
```sql
CURRENT_DATE = '2024-03-08'
```

# Task
Write a SQL query to identify all instances of customer re-engagement. For each re-engagement instance, calculate the average order value from all *prior* orders made by that customer (i.e., orders with an `order_date` strictly before the `re_engagement_order_date`). Then, classify the re-engagement as 'Successful' if the `re_engagement_order_value` is greater than or equal to this calculated average, otherwise classify it as 'Unsuccessful'.

The output should include the following columns for each re-engagement instance:
- `customer_id`
- `re_engagement_order_id`
- `re_engagement_order_date`
- `re_engagement_order_value`
- `avg_order_value_prior_to_re_engagement`
- `re_engagement_status` ('Successful' or 'Unsuccessful')

Only include customers who have at least one re-engagement instance.

# SQL Scenario-Based Interview Question: Identify 'At-Risk' Churned Customers for Re-Engagement

## Business Scenario
You are a Data Analyst for a subscription-based E-commerce company. The marketing team is planning a new, highly targeted re-engagement campaign for customers who have previously churned. They need your help to identify a specific segment of "At-Risk" customers. These are customers who had an active subscription, then cancelled, were offered a 'Win-Back Discount' promotion after their churn, but have *not* re-subscribed with a new active subscription since that win-back offer was made. The team wants to understand who these customers are and how long they've been churned to tailor their messaging.

Assume:
```sql
CURRENT_DATE = '2023-10-26'
```

---

# Database Setup

## Create Subscriptions Table
```sql
CREATE TABLE Subscriptions (
    subscription_id VARCHAR(50) PRIMARY KEY,
    customer_id VARCHAR(50) NOT NULL,
    plan_id VARCHAR(50) NOT NULL,
    start_date DATE NOT NULL,
    end_date DATE, -- NULL if currently active
    status VARCHAR(20) NOT NULL -- 'active', 'cancelled'
);
```

# Task
Identify unique customers who are currently considered "At-Risk" based on the following criteria:
1.  The customer has had at least one subscription that eventually ended with a 'cancelled' status.
2.  Their *latest* subscription ended with a `status = 'cancelled'`.
3.  They received at least one 'Win-Back Discount' promotion *after* the `end_date` of their *latest* cancelled subscription.
4.  They have *not* re-subscribed with a new active subscription *after* the `offer_date` of the first 'Win-Back Discount' promotion they received (as per criterion 3) and *before* `CURRENT_DATE`.

For these customers, return their `customer_id`, the `latest_churn_date` (which is the `end_date` of their latest cancelled subscription), the `winback_offer_date` (which is the `offer_date` of the *first* 'Win-Back Discount' promotion they received after their `latest_churn_date`), and `time_since_churn_days` (the difference in days between `CURRENT_DATE` and `latest_churn_date`).

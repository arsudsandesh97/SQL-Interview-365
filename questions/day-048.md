# SQL Scenario-Based Interview Question: SaaS Churn Attribution after Lifecycle Events

## Business Scenario
A rapidly growing SaaS company, 'CloudSync', offers various subscription tiers and free trials. They've noticed fluctuating churn rates and suspect that certain critical lifecycle events might be key drivers of early churn. The Head of Product wants to understand the immediate impact of these events on user retention.

Specifically, they want to identify the percentage of users who churn within 30 days of either:
1.  **Upgrading** from a lower-tier paid plan to a higher-tier paid plan.
2.  **Downgrading** from a higher-tier paid plan to a lower-tier paid plan.
3.  **Converting** from a free trial to any paid plan.

Assume:
```sql
CURRENT_DATE = '2023-10-26'
```

## Create subscriptions Table
```sql
CREATE TABLE subscriptions (
    subscription_id INT PRIMARY KEY,
    user_id VARCHAR(50) NOT NULL,
    plan_id VARCHAR(50) NOT NULL,
    start_date DATE NOT NULL,
    end_date DATE, -- NULL if subscription is currently active
    FOREIGN KEY (user_id) REFERENCES users(user_id),
    FOREIGN KEY (plan_id) REFERENCES plans(plan_id)
);
```

# Task
Calculate the total number of unique users who experienced each of the three target event types (Trial Conversion, Upgrade, Downgrade), and the percentage of those users who subsequently churned within 30 days of that event.

A user is considered to have 'churned after an event' if:
1.  They experienced one of the target events, meaning a new subscription (`current_sub`) started.
2.  `current_sub` has an `end_date`.
3.  The duration of `current_sub` (from `start_date` to `end_date`, inclusive) is less than or equal to 30 days.
4.  There is no *further paid* subscription for this `user_id` starting within 30 days of `current_sub.end_date`. (Note: `end_date + 30 days` means up to and including the 30th day after `end_date`).

Exclude any events where the `current_sub` does not have an `end_date` (i.e., is still active).
Order the output by `event_type`.

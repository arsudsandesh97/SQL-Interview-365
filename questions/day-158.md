# SQL Scenario-Based Interview Question: Identifying SaaS Customer Churn Risk from Feature Usage Decline

## Business Scenario
You are a Data Analyst at a SaaS company that provides business intelligence tools. Your product offers various features, and one of the most critical is the "Dashboard Analytics" module. The Product and Customer Success teams are concerned about customer churn and believe that a significant drop in the usage of core features like "Dashboard Analytics" is an early indicator of customers being at risk.

Your task is to identify customers who exhibit a significant decline in their "Dashboard Analytics" feature usage, so the Customer Success team can proactively reach out to them.

Assume:
```sql
CURRENT_DATE = '2023-10-26'
```

# Task
Write a SQL query to identify customers at risk of churn based on their "Dashboard Analytics" feature usage. A customer is considered "at risk" if their average daily usage of 'Dashboard Analytics' in the *last 30 days* is less than 50% of their average daily usage of 'Dashboard Analytics' in the *preceding 90 days*.

**Conditions:**
1.  The analysis should focus solely on the 'Dashboard Analytics' feature.
2.  The "last 30 days" period ends on `CURRENT_DATE`. So, `CURRENT_DATE - 29 days` to `CURRENT_DATE`. (e.g., for `2023-10-26`, this is `2023-09-27` to `2023-10-26`).
3.  The "preceding 90 days" period immediately precedes the "last 30 days" period. So, `CURRENT_DATE - 30 days - 89 days` to `CURRENT_DATE - 30 days`. (e.g., for `2023-10-26`, this is `2023-06-29` to `2023-09-26`).
4.  Average daily usage should be calculated as `SUM(usage_count) / [number of days in the period]`.
5.  Only include customers who had at least some usage (`SUM(usage_count) > 0`) of 'Dashboard Analytics' in *both* the last 30 days and the preceding 90 days to ensure meaningful comparison.

The output should include `customer_id`, `avg_usage_last_30_days`, `avg_usage_prev_90_days`, and the `usage_decline_ratio` (calculated as `avg_usage_last_30_days / avg_usage_prev_90_days`), rounded to two decimal places. Order the results by `customer_id`.

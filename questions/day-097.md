# SQL Scenario-Based Interview Question: SaaS Customer Monthly Active Plan and MRR Reconstruction

## Business Scenario
You are a Data Analyst at a fast-growing SaaS company. The marketing and finance teams are struggling to get an accurate view of Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) and customer lifetime value. Their existing `subscription_events` table captures every single customer action (subscribe, upgrade, downgrade, cancel) with an `effective_date`. The challenge is that a customer can change plans multiple times, and cancellations often have a future `effective_date` (e.g., cancel today, but service ends at the end of the billing cycle next month).

Your task is to reconstruct the *actual active subscription plan* for each customer for every month they were subscribed, and calculate the corresponding monthly MRR based on that active plan. The goal is to provide a clean, month-by-month view of each customer's subscription journey and the revenue associated with it.

Assume:
```sql
CURRENT_DATE = '2023-11-15'
```

    signup_date DATE

## Create plan_tiers Table
```sql

    plan_id VARCHAR(50) PRIMARY KEY,
    plan_name VARCHAR(100),
    monthly_price DECIMAL(10, 2)

```

## Create subscription_events Table
```sql
CREATE TABLE subscription_events (
    event_id SERIAL PRIMARY KEY,
    customer_id VARCHAR(50) REFERENCES customers(customer_id),
    event_type VARCHAR(50), -- e.g., 'SUBSCRIBE', 'UPGRADE', 'DOWNGRADE', 'CANCEL'
    new_plan_id VARCHAR(50) REFERENCES plan_tiers(plan_id), -- NULL for CANCEL events
    event_timestamp TIMESTAMP, -- When the action was recorded
    effective_date DATE -- When the plan change or cancellation becomes active
);
```

# Task
Generate a monthly report showing each customer's active subscription plan and the corresponding MRR for every month they were subscribed, from their first subscription `effective_date` up to and including the month of their last active service date.

For each `customer_id` and `subscription_month` (start of the month), determine:
1.  The `active_plan_name` they were on during that month.
2.  The `monthly_mrr` based on that plan.

**Conditions:**
*   A customer is considered active on a plan in a given `subscription_month` if the `effective_date` of that plan (or a prior plan) falls within or before the `subscription_month`, and the end date of that plan's segment is within or after the `subscription_month`.
*   The actual `end_date` for a subscription segment is derived as follows:
    *   If the *next* event for the customer is an `UPGRADE` or `DOWNGRADE`, the current plan segment ends the day *before* the `effective_date` of the next event.
    *   If the *current* event is a `CANCEL` event, its `effective_date` signifies the end of the *previously active* plan for that customer.
    *   If a plan starts and there are no subsequent events (or the last event isn't a CANCEL that closes it off), assume it continues indefinitely up to `CURRENT_DATE`.
*   The `subscription_month` should be the first day of the month (e.g., '2023-01-01').
*   Order the results by `customer_id` and then `subscription_month`.

# SQL Scenario-Based Interview Question: Complementary Product Co-Purchase Analysis

## Business Scenario
You are a Data Analyst at "GadgetGrove," a fast-growing e-commerce company specializing in electronics and accessories. The marketing team is planning new cross-promotional campaigns and product bundling strategies. They need your help to identify strong co-purchase relationships between products. Specifically, they want to find pairs of products where one is a popular "anchor" product, and the other is a "complementary" product frequently bought alongside it in the same order, even if the complementary product isn't a top seller on its own. This insight will guide "Frequently Bought Together" recommendations and potential bundle creations.

Assume:
```sql
CURRENT_DATE = '2023-10-26'
```

# Task
Generate a list of complementary product pairs based on co-purchase behavior.

For each pair of products (Product A, Product B) where Product A is considered the "anchor" product and Product B is the "complementary" product, you need to calculate the `Co_Purchase_Rate`. This rate is defined as:

`Co_Purchase_Rate = (Number of distinct orders containing BOTH Product A AND Product B) / (Total number of distinct orders containing Product A)`

Apply the following conditions:
1.  Consider only orders placed *on or after October 1, 2023*.
2.  Each identified pair must have `Product_A_ID < Product_B_ID` to ensure unique pairs (e.g., if (Laptop, Mouse) is included, (Mouse, Laptop) should not be).
3.  Product A must have appeared in at least 5 distinct orders within the specified date range.
4.  There must be at least 3 distinct orders where Product A and Product B were purchased together within the specified date range.
5.  The calculated `Co_Purchase_Rate` must be at least 0.5 (50%).
6.  Order the final results by `Co_Purchase_Rate` in descending order.

The output should include the names of `Product A`, `Product B`, their calculated `Co_Purchase_Rate` (formatted to two decimal places), the `Orders_with_A_and_B`, and `Total_Orders_with_A`.

# SQL Scenario-Based Interview Question: Identifying Persistent Delivery Delays by Region and Driver

## Business Scenario
SpeedyShip Logistics aims to improve customer satisfaction and operational efficiency by proactively identifying and addressing persistent delivery delays. As a Data Analyst, you've been tasked with analyzing recent delivery data to pinpoint specific delivery regions and drivers who consistently deliver packages beyond their expected delivery windows. This analysis will help the operations team focus their training and route optimization efforts.

Assume:
```sql
CURRENT_DATE = '2023-10-26'
```

---

# Database Setup

## Create deliveries Table
```sql
CREATE TABLE deliveries (
    delivery_id VARCHAR(50) PRIMARY KEY,
    package_id VARCHAR(50) NOT NULL UNIQUE,
    driver_id VARCHAR(50) NOT NULL,
    delivery_region VARCHAR(100) NOT NULL,
    scheduled_pickup_time DATETIME NOT NULL,
    actual_delivery_time DATETIME, -- NULL if not yet delivered
    expected_delivery_start DATETIME NOT NULL,
    expected_delivery_end DATETIME NOT NULL
);
```

# Task
Identify all combinations of `delivery_region` and `driver_id` that meet the following criteria:
1.  They have completed at least **5 deliveries** in the last 30 days (up to and including `CURRENT_DATE`).
2.  Their *average delivery delay* beyond the `expected_delivery_end` time is greater than **30 minutes**.
    *   A delivery is considered delayed only if `actual_delivery_time` is later than `expected_delivery_end`.
    *   The delay should be calculated as the difference in minutes between `actual_delivery_time` and `expected_delivery_end`.
    *   For deliveries that are on-time or early, their delay value should be considered 0 for the average calculation.

Output the `delivery_region`, `driver_id`, `total_deliveries_last_30_days`, and `average_delay_minutes` (rounded to 2 decimal places). Order the results by `average_delay_minutes` in descending order.

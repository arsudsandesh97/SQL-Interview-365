# SQL Scenario-Based Interview Question: Identifying High-Impact Complementary Product Pairs

## Business Scenario
You are a Data Analyst at "GadgetGrove", a fast-growing e-commerce platform specializing in electronics and tech accessories. The marketing team is looking to optimize their "Frequently Bought Together" recommendation engine. Currently, the recommendations are based on simple co-occurrence (products appearing in the same order), which often suggests generic bundles without considering the temporal aspect of customer journeys.

The team wants to identify "high-impact complementary product pairs" – meaning Product B is bought by a customer *within 7 days* after they first purchased Product A, and this conditional purchase behavior for Product B is significantly higher than Product B's general purchase rate across all customers. This insight will help tailor more timely and effective upsell/cross-sell recommendations.

Assume:
```sql
CURRENT_DATE = '2023-10-26'
```

# Task
Identify "high-impact complementary product pairs" (Product A, Product B) based on the following criteria:

1.  **Product A and Product B must be distinct.**
2.  A customer purchases Product A (first instance of this product for this customer) on `date_A`.
3.  The *same customer* then purchases Product B (first instance of this product for this customer) on `date_B`.
4.  `date_B` must be *strictly after* `date_A` and `date_B` must be within 7 days of `date_A` (i.e., `date_B <= date_A + 7 days`).
5.  Calculate `P(B|A, 7_days)`: The probability that a customer buys Product B within 7 days *after* first buying Product A. This is defined as:
    `(Number of unique customers who bought Product A then Product B within 7 days) / (Total number of unique customers who bought Product A)`
6.  Calculate `P(B)`: The baseline probability of a customer buying Product B (regardless of Product A or timeframe). This is defined as:
    `(Total number of unique customers who bought Product B) / (Total number of unique customers in the dataset)`
7.  A pair `(Product A, Product B)` is considered "high-impact complementary" if:
    *   `P(B|A, 7_days)` is strictly greater than `P(B)`.
    *   `P(B|A, 7_days)` is at least `0.10` (to filter out very rare occurrences).

Your query should return `product_name_A`, `product_name_B`, `P_B_given_A_7_days`, and `P_B_baseline`, sorted by `P_B_given_A_7_days` in descending order. Probabilities should be rounded to 2 decimal places.

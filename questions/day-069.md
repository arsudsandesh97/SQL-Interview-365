# SQL Scenario-Based Interview Question: SaaS Customer Churn vs. Plan Transition Analysis

## Business Scenario
A rapidly growing SaaS company, 'CloudFlow Solutions', offers various subscription plans to its B2B customers. The marketing and product teams are struggling to accurately measure customer churn because a significant number of customers don't truly leave; instead, they might cancel one plan only to immediately subscribe to a different plan (upgrade or downgrade), or even resubscribe to the same plan after a very short break. This internal movement skews traditional churn metrics. You are a Data Analyst tasked with providing a more nuanced view of customer departure and plan changes for a specific reporting month.

Assume:
```sql
CURRENT_DATE = '2023-06-01' -- Analysis for the month of May 2023
```

## Create `customer_subscriptions` Table
```sql
CREATE TABLE customer_subscriptions (
    subscription_id VARCHAR(50) PRIMARY KEY,
    customer_id VARCHAR(50),
    plan_id VARCHAR(50),
    start_date DATE,
    end_date DATE NULL, -- NULL if currently active
    FOREIGN KEY (customer_id) REFERENCES customers(customer_id),
    FOREIGN KEY (plan_id) REFERENCES subscription_plans(plan_id)
);
```

# Task
For the reporting month of May 2023 (i.e., `CURRENT_DATE = '2023-06-01'`, so we are interested in subscriptions with an `end_date` in May), classify each customer who had a subscription *end* in May 2023 into one of the following categories:

1.  **'True Churn'**: A customer's *last active* subscription ended in May 2023, and they *did not* start any new subscription (regardless of `plan_id`) within 30 days following that `end_date`.
2.  **'Plan Transition'**: A customer's subscription ended in May 2023, and they *did* start a new subscription to a *different* plan within 30 days following that `end_date`.
3.  **'Resubscribed (Same Plan)'**: A customer's subscription ended in May 2023, and they *did* start a new subscription to the *same* plan within 30 days following that `end_date`.

If a customer had multiple subscriptions end in May, consider only the `end_date` of their *last* ending subscription within May for classification. However, the subsequent subscription check within the 30-day window should consider *any* new subscription start for that customer, regardless of its previous association.

The output should list `customer_id`, the `end_date` of their last subscription that ended in May 2023, and their `customer_category`.

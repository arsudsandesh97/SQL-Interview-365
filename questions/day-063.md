# SQL Scenario-Based Interview Question: Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) Change Analysis

## Business Scenario
A rapidly growing SaaS company wants to deeply understand the dynamics of its Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR). Specifically, for the *previous full month*, they need to calculate the total MRR change broken down into its key components: New MRR (from new subscriptions), Churn MRR (from cancellations), Upgrade MRR (from plan upgrades), and Downgrade MRR (from plan downgrades). This report is crucial for financial forecasting and strategic decision-making.

Assume:
```sql
CURRENT_DATE = '2023-07-15'
```
(This means you should analyze the data for **June 2023**)

# Database Setup

## Create subscriptions Table
```sql
CREATE TABLE subscriptions (
    subscription_id INT PRIMARY KEY,
    customer_id INT NOT NULL,
    plan_id VARCHAR(50) NOT NULL,
    start_date DATE NOT NULL,
    end_date DATE, -- NULL if subscription is currently active, otherwise the last day it was active
    mrr_amount DECIMAL(10, 2) NOT NULL
);
```

# Task
Calculate the total MRR impact for the month of **June 2023** (based on `CURRENT_DATE = '2023-07-15'`), categorized as 'New MRR', 'Churn MRR', 'Upgrade MRR', and 'Downgrade MRR'.

Define the categories as follows:
*   **New MRR:** Sum of `mrr_amount` for customers who had no active subscription on `2023-05-31` but had an active subscription on `2023-06-30`.
*   **Churn MRR:** Sum of `mrr_amount` (from their previous plan) for customers who had an active subscription on `2023-05-31` but had no active subscription on `2023-06-30`. This should be reported as a *negative* value.
*   **Upgrade MRR:** Sum of the *increase* in `mrr_amount` for customers who had active subscriptions on both `2023-05-31` and `2023-06-30`, and their MRR increased during June.
*   **Downgrade MRR:** Sum of the *decrease* in `mrr_amount` for customers who had active subscriptions on both `2023-05-31` and `2023-06-30`, and their MRR decreased during June. This should be reported as a *negative* value.

A subscription is considered active on a specific `DATE_X` if `start_date <= DATE_X` AND (`end_date IS NULL` OR `end_date >= DATE_X`). If a customer has multiple overlapping subscriptions, assume the one with the latest `start_date` that is active on `DATE_X` is the primary. (However, the provided `subscriptions` data is structured such that a customer only has one effective active subscription at any given point, making the "latest `start_date`" rule implicitly handled.)

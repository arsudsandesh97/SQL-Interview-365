# SQL Scenario-Based Interview Question: Identifying High-Value Customers with Significant Recent Purchase Frequency Drop

## Business Scenario
As a Senior Data Analyst at 'TrendPulse E-commerce', your marketing team is focused on customer retention. They've observed that merely targeting customers based on their last purchase date isn't enough; some previously active, high-value customers might still be making occasional purchases but their overall buying frequency has drastically declined, signaling a potential churn risk. The team wants to identify these specific customers for a targeted re-engagement campaign. They need a list of customers who *were* frequent buyers but have recently slowed down significantly, even if they're not yet fully inactive.

Assume:
```sql
CURRENT_DATE = '2023-10-26'
```

# Task
Write a SQL query to identify `customer_id`s who meet the following criteria, indicating a significant drop in purchase frequency:

1.  **"Previous Period" Activity:** The customer made at least 3 purchases in the 30-day period immediately preceding the "Current Period".
    *   *Previous Period:* From `CURRENT_DATE - INTERVAL '60 days'` to `CURRENT_DATE - INTERVAL '31 days'` (inclusive).
    *   Using `CURRENT_DATE = '2023-10-26'`, this is `2023-08-28` to `2023-09-26`.
2.  **"Current Period" Drop:** Their purchase frequency (number of orders) in the most recent 30-day period ("Current Period") dropped by at least 50% compared to their frequency in the "Previous Period".
    *   *Current Period:* From `CURRENT_DATE - INTERVAL '30 days'` to `CURRENT_DATE` (inclusive).
    *   Using `CURRENT_DATE = '2023-10-26'`, this is `2023-09-27` to `2023-10-26`.
3.  **Recent Activity:** The customer's absolute latest order date (across all time) must be within the last 90 days from `CURRENT_DATE`. This ensures we target recently active, but slowing down, customers, not those who churned long ago.
    *   Using `CURRENT_DATE = '2023-10-26'`, this is from `2023-07-28` to `2023-10-26`.

Calculate the `purchase_drop_percentage` as `((previous_period_purchases - current_period_purchases) / previous_period_purchases) * 100` and round it to 2 decimal places.

Return `customer_id` and `purchase_drop_percentage`.
